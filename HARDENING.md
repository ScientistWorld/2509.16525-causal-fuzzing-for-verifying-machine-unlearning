# Hardening report (final iteration)

## Verdict

**Better results score higher, and the major shortcuts do not pay. The gym now also scores the paper's real data.**

This iteration fixed what `REVIEW.md` found still weak in the already-hardened gym:

1. **Only synthetic structural causal models were scored.** A fourth scored experiment, `adult_feature_unlearning`, is built on the paper's real UCI Adult Income data. It is semi-synthetic only where exact ground truth requires it. The reference and both baselines are run on it.
2. **The reference sat at its bound on every main detection metric**, so headroom lived only in the error metrics. The new experiment's primary metric is the verification decision itself. The reference scores 0.953 on it, not 1.0, and an honest variant at the same query cost reaches 0.972.

Numbers below come from the final run:
- 28 verifiers, each run once through the hidden test. The test has 48 tasks: 12 Heart-Disease, 12 Performance and 24 Adult-Income.
- The 13 honest verifiers were also run through the visible train slice (48 tasks).
- Raw answers were cached and rescored with the final evaluator.
- Results are in `notes/hardening/results/`: `rank_summary.md`, per-task records and raw answers. `python notes/hardening/rank.py` (pure Python) recomputes every score.
- `scoring/scores_{train,test}.json` and `scoring/scores.json` were regenerated by `scripts/evaluate_*.sh` from the final code, with only the reference verifiers registered.
- `python validate.py --compare --post-design` passes.

**How "score vs reference" is computed.** It follows the gym's rule:

- For each experiment and metric, take the experiment weight × |coefficient| × the improvement in the metric's better direction.
- Divide by that metric's population standard deviation over the ranked cohort.
- CAFÉ, the reference, scores 0.

95% confidence intervals come from a paired bootstrap over held-out tasks; every method sees the same tasks. Two cohorts are reported:
- **all**: all 28 hardening runs;
- **honest+**: the 13 honest runs, plus the one shortcut being scored (for shortcut rows).

## What changed in this iteration

### 1. Real-data experiment `adult_feature_unlearning` (REVIEW item 1)

The paper evaluates CAFÉ on Adult Income. It uses the causal graph of Chiappa (2019): age, sex, race, native country → marital status → education → work class, occupation → hours → income. It reports the influence ranking (Fig. 4b), its stability across logistic regression, random forest and neural networks (Fig. 8), and runtime (Table 3). It has no ground truth.

Here the real data are kept wherever exact ground truth allows:

| Part | Visible slice (`data/adult_semisynthetic.py`) | Hidden slice (`eval/test/adult.py`) |
|---|---|---|
| Real rows | UCI `adult.data` (30,162 complete rows) | UCI `adult.test` (15,060 complete rows) |
| Root attributes (age, sex, race, native country) | drawn from their real marginals, independent as the graph states | same |
| Noise of the count attributes (education years, weekly hours) | the real rows' standardized residuals | same, with heteroscedastic scale |
| Mechanisms of married, education, self-employment, professional/managerial job, hours, income label | logistic / linear regression on a quadratic basis, fitted on the real rows | gradient boosting, fitted on the real rows |

Per task, the evaluator varies the paths of the three prohibited attributes:
- **sex** (the unlearning target) always has its real paths, rescaled.
- **race** (the control) has no path in a third of the tasks, otherwise moderate or strong paths.
- **native country** has no path, moderate paths or strong paths.

Moderate paths put the residual influence around the verification thresholds. Random-sign path shifts make mediated influences partly cancel.

Each task then works like the existing families:
- Income labels are drawn from the fitted outcome mechanism.
- The audited classifier is trained without the prohibited attributes. It is one of logistic regression, random forest, gradient boosting or MLP, as in the paper's Fig. 8, and its output is P(income > 50K).
- In a third of the tasks sex stays an input, a failed unlearning.
- Ground truth comes from counterfactuals propagated with each row's own noise.
- Each slice has 24 tasks with 15,000 rows each, 3,500 of them audit rows, and the same 2-rows-per-audit-row oracle budget.

The generated data match the real marginals. On the visible canonical task: sex 0.67 vs 0.67, married 0.45 vs 0.47, education 10.0 vs 10.1, professional job 0.26 vs 0.26.

`scripts/download.sh` fetches both UCI files with a real `curl` command, verifies their sha256, and skips files already present.

**Metrics.** The Adult experiment uses the same metrics as the other feature experiments, plus `verdict_accuracy` as its primary metric (coefficient 1.0). This is the paper's own decision (Def. 4 / Problem 1): is each prohibited attribute's residual influence above a regulator's threshold?
- It is judged at thresholds of 5%, 10%, 20% and 30% of the task scale.
- At threshold t the evaluator flags the attribute when the reported |total| ≥ t × scale.
- The score is balanced accuracy over (attribute, threshold) pairs.
- Pairs within 1% of a threshold are not scored.

**Weights.** Adult gets 0.25. The other experiments are scaled by 0.75: 0.40 / 0.35 / 0.25 → 0.30 / 0.26 / 0.19. Real data is three of the paper's five datasets, and Adult carries its real-data ranking, architecture and efficiency results.

### 3. Other changes

- **No load-dependent limit in the method's path.** Verifiers used to run under a 3-hour wall-clock timeout. They now run under a CPU-time limit (`ulimit -t`, 3 CPU-hours per process), which does not depend on machine load. A 5-hour wall-clock backstop only ends verifiers that hang idle; the reference needs about 6 minutes per slice.
- **Robust intervention values.** If an attribute's quartiles coincide, the evaluator falls back to the 10th/90th percentiles, then to min/max.
- **Hardening tools.**
  - `variants.py`: binary mediators now get threshold abduction in the counterfactual variants. New honest variant `full_adjust`: CAFÉ with every non-descendant as the adjustment set.
  - `shortcuts.py`: two new shortcuts, `cafe_assume_unlearned` and `cafe_shrink_unlearned`.
  - `run_hardening.sh`: writes the official score files and validates first, restores them after the score-file forgery shortcut runs, and resumes cleanly; the scheduler preempted the jobs several times.

## Left open

- **`shortcut_cafe_zero_direct_gap`: +0.136 [+0.019, +0.255] (all), +0.039 [−0.269, +0.349] (honest+).** Unchanged from the previous iteration; the Heart subgroup tasks were not touched.
  - It is CAFÉ, but it gives both age groups CAFÉ's population direct effect. This is the paper's "direct effects identical" finding, assumed rather than measured.
  - It exposes that CAFÉ's per-group direct estimates are worse than that assumption. It ties among honest runs.
  - Honest per-group measurement beats it by far: direct-gap error 0.008 for causal fuzzing vs 0.256 for CAFÉ.
  - Closing it would mean making direct gaps large enough that the paper's own claim fails, so it is left to the shortcut grader.
- **`shortcut_cafe_shrink_unlearned`.** After the redesign it ties: +0.005 against all runs, interval [−0.029, +0.036], below the reference seed spread of 0.020. What gain remains comes from exact-zero controls in the original Heart / Performance tasks: Performance `direct_effect_nmae`. Removing those exact zeros would remove the false-alarm constraint those experiments need. It is out of bounds in `scoring/DIRECTION.md` and left to the grader.
- **Ties as the reference's own estimates.** `shortcut_forged_fields` and `shortcut_scores_file_forgery` submit CAFÉ's estimates plus junk and score exactly 0.
- **Public real data.** The UCI Adult files are public, and the visible family's construction is public code. A verifier that reads `data/downloads/` could fit structural models on all 45k real rows instead of the 3,500 audit rows. The hidden per-task path strengths and the different hidden mechanism models limit what that buys, but it was not measured. `scoring/CONSTRAINTS.md` forbids reading `data/downloads/` at run time; it is left to the grader.

## Headroom

The reference sits at the bound of:
- `residual_capture` = 1.0 in all three feature experiments;
- `false_alarm_rate` = 0 in all three (both are constraints);
- `query_budget_fraction` = 0.5, the one-pass floor;
- `indirect_gap_direction` = 1.0.

It sits below the bound, with honest variants measured above it, on:
- `verdict_accuracy` (Adult primary): 0.953 vs 0.972 for `full_adjust` and 0.955 for `abduction`;
- `rank_spearman`:

  | Experiment | CAFÉ | Best honest variant |
  |---|---:|---|
  | Adult | 0.976 | 0.982 (`hgb`) |
  | Heart | 0.938 | 0.943 (`abduction_half`) |
  | Performance | 0.983 | 0.992 (`abduction_half`) |

- every error metric;
- subgroup capture: 0.673 vs 0.96.

The Adult experiment was added for headroom and coverage where the paper's method falls short. On real data and a near-threshold regulatory decision, CAFÉ's unadjusted back-door contrast for the root attributes is noisy enough to flip verdicts.

**Brute force and baselines.**
- Exhaustive causal fuzzing needs 4 × 9 rows per audit row on Adult and is refused.
- Budget-limited fuzzing (the paper's baseline) ranks below the reference.
- The direct-only baseline is far below the reference on every main metric, with room above the reference.

## Could not fix / not scored

- **Ranking depends on the normalisation cohort.** For example, `variant_abduction` (same accuracy, twice the queries) ranks −1.39 against all runs and +0.05 among honest runs. `variant_hgb`'s all-runs interval touches 0.
- **Not scored:**
  - the paper's German Credit and Drug datasets;
  - graph-misspecification robustness (RQ3);
  - multi-feature cancellation (Drug);
  - the Adult age < 40 subgroup ranking (Sec. 5.3.2).
- **Validator warnings.** `validate.py` warns that the harnesses "look detached from the method". This is by design: the evaluator runs each verifier against its oracle and scores the raw answers.
- **Scheduler preemptions.** Jobs were preempted several times ("SLURM worker drain"). The driver resumes from cached per-verifier answers. Every reported number comes from one completed scoring pass with the final evaluator.

## Reproduce

- `bash scripts/download.sh`, then `bash scripts/run.sh`. This takes about 20 CPU-minutes. It runs the reference and both baselines on both slices and `validate.py`.
- `bash notes/hardening/run_hardening.sh` runs all variants and shortcuts. It reuses cached raw answers in `notes/hardening/results/{test,train}/` and rescores them with the current evaluator; delete those directories to rerun from scratch, about 2.5 CPU-hours.
- `python notes/hardening/adult_diag.py {train,test} OUT [verifier ...]` prints the Adult truth distribution and scores verifiers on the Adult tasks only.
- Earlier rounds are kept in `notes/hardening/results_round{1..6}*` and `notes/hardening/adult_calibration_round1/`.
